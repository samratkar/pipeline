The paper introduces a Semantic SPA (Student-Project Allocation) framework to address interoperability challenges in education that arise with globalization and cross-institution collaborations. SPA, traditionally about forming a stable matching between students and projects, becomes more complex when universities, campuses, and disciplines differ and resources outside a single institution may be required. The authors argue that leveraging Web 3.0 (semantic) technologies can standardize data and enable interoperable solutions across educational boundaries, capitalizing on semantic investments (e.g., institutions exposing data as Linked Open Data and semantically describing experts and projects).

The core contributions of the Semantic SPA framework are threefold:
- SPA ontology: A new OWL-based ontology that standardizes SPA concepts semantically, enabling consistent representation of students, projects, capacity constraints, and related entities across institutions.
- Semantic Web services: SOAP-based Web services that implement the SPA solution, designed to be reusable across different educational contexts and capable of being discovered and invoked in situ.
- OWL-S based orchestration: An OWL-S specification that enables automatic discovery, composition, and invocation of the SPA services by educational institutions or semantic agents on the Web, supporting situational SPA scenarios.

Key goals include formalizing SPA data elements using semantic Web concepts, providing a reference ontology for situational SPA, and adopting a semantic Web service approach to facilitate interoperability. The framework targets situational SPA problems arising from globalization, such as interdisciplinary research, intra- and inter-institution collaborations, and multi-campus universities, where expertise and opportunities may lie outside a single campus or discipline. By standardizing data and enabling automatic service discovery and composition, the Semantic SPA framework aims to achieve stable matchings in more diverse and distributed educational settings, overcoming the limitations of conventional, non-interoperable SPA solutions.
