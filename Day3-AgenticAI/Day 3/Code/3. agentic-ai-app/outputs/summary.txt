The paper addresses the Student-Project Allocation (SPA) problem, a classical task in which students are matched to final-year projects under capacity and constraint conditions. With globalization, universities face interoperability challenges: data about students, projects, and experts are spread across campuses and disciplines, making cross-institution or cross-campus allocations difficult. The authors argue that semantic Web 3.0 technologies can standardize and interlink such heterogeneous data, enabling scalable and reusable SPA solutions beyond a single institution.

To tackle this, they propose the Semantic SPA framework, which aims to leverage existing semantic investments in educational ecosystems. The core ideas are: (i) standardization of SPA data elements through a dedicated SPA ontology to capture concepts such as students, projects, experts, capabilities, and constraints in a machine-understandable form; (ii) a reference ontology specifically tailored for situational SPA, encompassing cross-institutional and cross-disciplinary scenarios; and (iii) a semantic Web service approach built on OWL-S to enable automatic discovery, composition, and invocation of SPA services across institutions.

Architecturally, the framework comprises: a) the SPA ontology that semantically encodes SPA concepts and relationships; b) SOAP-based Web services that implement SPA functionality (e.g., matching, allocation, and constraint handling); and c) OWL-S layered semantics that facilitate dynamic discovery and orchestration of these services by educational institutions or semantic agents on the Web. By combining these components, the framework supports situational SPA where external or remote expertise and resources can be brought into consideration, enabling stable matchings in more heterogeneous and globally distributed educational settings.

Overall, the paper contributes a cohesive Semantic SPA framework that (a) formalizes SPA data semantically, (b) provides a reference ontology for situational SPA, and (c) enables interoperable, service-driven realization of SPA through OWL-S, thereby addressing interoperability challenges and enabling automatic, cross-boundary allocation scenarios in higher education.
